# GATEFALL — Story & Design Bible (v2)

*Working title. An idle action-RPG × romance sim: an ordinary person who awakened, the otherworldly tenants who fight beside them, and the gates that keep tearing the sky open.*

---

## Logline

Three years ago, gates tore open across the world — unstable seams to countless other realities. Monsters poured through. So did refugees. And in a fraction of ordinary people, something *woke up*: mana, and the power to fight back. You're one of them — nobody special, just awakened. You run a rundown building, you take in the refugees the world has no room for, and you clear gates for the mana to grow stronger. The house you're building and the power you're earning are the same fight from two sides: making a place worth protecting, and becoming strong enough to protect it.

---

## The World

**The Gatefall.** Gates appeared without warning — not to one world, but to a **multiverse**. Each gate is a tear to a different reality, and no two spill the same things. A gate can't be permanently destroyed — seal one and the phenomenon simply tears open elsewhere — but an individual gate is **closed by clearing it**: fighting through in a **gate raid** and destroying its core. Left uncleared too long, a gate **destabilizes and surges**, flooding the area with more and stronger monsters. So clearing gates is both how you earn (Mana and loot) *and* how you keep the world from drowning.

**The Awakened.** When the gates came, a small fraction of humans changed — they developed **mana** and abilities. These Awakened are Earth's only real defense against what comes through. You are one of them: weak at first, one of many, no destiny attached. Your strength is *earned*, gate by gate.

**The Gatekin.** Not everything that crosses over is a monster. Refugees came too — **elves, beastkin, and stranger folk from many different worlds** — fleeing the same tide of monsters, or worse. Earth wasn't ready for them. Housing overflowed, paperwork failed, prejudice grew on both sides. Coexistence works most days. Barely.

**The deeper question.** Everyone treats the gates as weather — a disaster to endure. But gates don't just *happen*. Why did they start? Why now? What's actually on the far side of the ones no one dares enter? That mystery is the spine of the endgame — the thing behind the thing.

The shape of the game: cozy home-building and slow romance on one side of the door, escalating gate-combat on the other, and a slow-burn mystery pulling both toward a reckoning.

---

## The Player

An **ordinary person who awakened**. No chosen-one prophecy, no secret bloodline. You inherited a tired old building and a knack for mana you didn't ask for. You start *fragile* — early gates are genuinely dangerous — and you grow into a force by clearing them, upgrading your gear, and fighting alongside the tenants who become your team and, if you choose, more than that.

That growth-from-weakness is the core power fantasy. The player should *feel* the distance between the person who barely survived their first gate and the one who walks into a rift without flinching.

---

## The Cast

Four core companions plus one human foil. Each is a full character first, a party member second, and a romance route third — because romance is player-driven, the player should care about them even if they never pursue them. Several hail from **different worlds**.

### Faelen — Elf, ex-Warden *(frontline)*
Stoic, watchful, sworn once to guard the gates of her own world — and failed. Fights like it's penance.
- **Secret:** She crossed over chasing the tide of monsters, meaning to finish a duty that got people killed. Terrified of pulling the house into her war.
- **In battle:** Disciplined melee anchor — the wall the rest of the party fights behind.
- **Arc:** Protecting people doesn't have to mean pushing them away.

### Kess — Fox Beastkin, the hustler *(fast DPS)*
Loud, quick, magnetic. Adapted to Earth faster than anyone — streams, side-gigs, always broke.
- **Secret:** Every coin goes toward finding family she was separated from in the crossing. She doesn't know if they made it.
- **In battle:** Darting, high-speed striker — big damage, fragile if caught.
- **Arc:** Letting the house *be* the family she's been searching for.

### Momo — Gloamkin, the quiet one *(mage / support)*
Small, bookish, whisper-soft. Can *feel* gates and the wrongness that leaks from them.
- **Secret:** Her sensitivity draws the worst things toward her. She may be the key to the mystery — or its target.
- **In battle:** Ranged spellcaster; her gate-sense powers utility and warnings.
- **Arc:** Being needed and being a burden are not the same thing.

### Thora — Orc-kin, the heart *(tank / healer)*
Tall, warm, a former healer and clan-mother who lost her people in the crossing. Mothers everyone whether they want it or not.
- **Secret:** She doesn't believe she deserves a second home.
- **In battle:** Bulwark and mender — keeps the party standing.
- **Arc:** Letting herself be cared for instead of only caring.

### Dana — Human, the foil *(non-combat / late ally)*
A government integration caseworker assigned to your building. By-the-book, tired, suspicious. Voices the world's prejudice so your tenants don't have to — and softens as the world does.
- **Why she matters:** The grounded human romance option, and a window into the institutional side of coexistence. Could awaken late as a wildcard combatant.

*Names, species, and combat roles are placeholders. The skeleton — personality, secret, arc, battle role — is the part that matters.*

## Core Gameplay Loop

**Combat is an idle auto-battler.** You pick an active gate and send your party in; they fight automatically while you **tap to trigger abilities and ultimates** at the right moments. Low-friction to play, satisfying to optimize. This is your equivalent of Cozy Landlord Life's money loop — except it's the beating heart of the game, not filler.

**Gates rotate.** Gates are closed by clearing them (or destabilize and surge if ignored), so the available gates are always changing — new ones tear open, cleared ones close. That gives you a natural, built-in content refresh and a real reason to come back: today's gates aren't tomorrow's, and an ignored gate becomes tomorrow's bigger problem.

**Your party fights with you.** You + your companions form the team. Each has a combat role (see cast). Building your roster *is* building your relationships — the people you get close to are the people watching your back.

### Progression tracks
- **Player:** level up, unlock and upgrade your own mana abilities, equip and improve gear.
- **Companions:** raise each one's combat level and skills, in their role.
- **Gear:** drops from monsters and gates; upgrade, enchant, and craft better equipment over time.

## Economy — Two Currencies + One Stat

The economy *is* the theme. Two wallets, pulling opposite directions:

### Mana — the currency of power *(the world outside the door)*
Harvested from slain monsters. Spent on everything that makes you stronger:
- Leveling companions' combat power and skills
- Unlocking and upgrading your own abilities
- Enchanting and upgrading gear

### Gold — the currency of home *(the world inside the door)*
Ordinary Earth money from **rent**, selling monster drops and materials, and odd jobs. Spent on the domestic and romantic side:
- House and room upgrades (more rooms = more tenants = more story)
- Furniture and comforts
- Gifts and date activities

### Bond — a per-companion stat, not a wallet
You don't buy Bond; you earn it — through gifts, dates, dialogue choices, and **fighting side by side**. Bond does double duty:
- It **gates the romance** — story beats, deeper truths, and endings unlock as it rises.
- It **buffs her in battle** — a close companion fights harder.

That last point is the keystone: **romance makes your party stronger.** The cozy side feeds the combat side instead of competing with it, so a player who invests in relationships is rewarded on the battlefield, and a player who grinds gates is pulled toward the people they're fighting for. The two halves of the game keep handing off to each other.

### Idle hooks
Mana should accrue passively while you're away (cleared gates keep yielding, companions "patrol"), so there's offline income to come back to — the core habit-forming loop of the genre, minus the ad-spam that sinks its competitors.

## Character Acquisition & Story Unlock

Obtaining a companion happens in **three layers**, and the middle one is where the landlord fantasy lives.

**1. Encounter — how you first meet them.**
Each companion has a scripted first meeting (their Beat 0), triggered by **player level + story act**, sometimes tied to a specific gate-world. The triggers differ by personality so acquisition never feels like one repeated action — you find Faelen wounded after a raid, Momo hiding near unstable gates, while Kess walks up to *you*. Surface who's out there on a "gate rumors" board or quest log so the player always has a target.

**2. Settle — how you actually obtain them.**
Meeting isn't housing. To recruit, you need a **vacant room** (built with Gold) *and* a short **recruitment quest** — solve their immediate problem (clear the gate menacing Momo, cover Kess's debt). Early on you'll **meet people faster than you can house them**, because capacity is limited by Gold. That gap is the pull, and it's Gold's main sink — the landlord fantasy, expressed as a system. Once settled, they join your party and Beat 1 opens.

**3. Unlock — their story, ongoing.**
The 7-beat route unlocks by raising **Bond**, fed by both pillars: fighting together in raids (Mana side) plus gifts and dates (Gold side). Beats gate on **Bond tier**, with the big ones also gated on **story act** — Beats 1–3 on Bond 1–3; **Beat 4 (the Fracture) on Bond 4 + Act 2**; **Beat 6 (the Choice) on Bond 6 + Act 3** — so the player controls the *pace* but the story controls the *timing* of the emotional peaks.

### The core five (guaranteed via story)

No missable main characters — everyone meets all five and sees the main plot. Bond investment is the optional, player-driven part. *(All levels and Gold costs are tunable placeholders.)*

| Character | First encounter | Recruitment requirement | Room / Gold |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------------|-------------|
| **Faelen** | Act 1 prologue, Lv 1 — story (your first gate collapse) | Tutorial: survive the opening raid | Free (starter room) |
| **Thora** | Act 1, ~Lv 5 — story (she adopts the house) | Take her in | Room 2 (~500 Gold) |
| **Kess** | Act 1, ~Lv 8 — social (she approaches you for gate footage) | Quest: settle her debt trouble | Room 3 (~1,500 Gold) |
| **Momo** | Act 1→2, ~Lv 12 — exploration (found near unstable gates) | Quest: clear the gate menacing her | Room 4 (~4,000 Gold) |
| **Dana** | Act 1, ~Lv 3 — story (assigned as your caseworker; foil, not yet a tenant) | Recruitable after her Beat 2 cover (Act 2); fully settles/awakens Act 3 | Moves in Act 2 (special, no standard room cost) |

Dana is the deliberate exception — she's present early as a foil, not a tenant, and "settles" gradually. Use her pattern for any late-joining or non-standard character.

### Future: gacha characters (bonus roster)

When gacha is added, a pull **does not** make a character instantly playable. The **first** time you pull a new character, a **character-specific unlock quest** appears — a short story mission themed to who they are, functioning as their Beat 0. Only on completing it do they settle into the house and their route begins. Later duplicate pulls convert to upgrade shards/materials.

Why this is the right call:
- It turns every pull into a **story event**, not a stat card, keeping gacha characters consistent with the "meet → settle → bond" grammar of the core cast.
- It gives paying players a reason to actually **play the content** they unlocked rather than auto-battling past it.
- It protects the tone — even a monetized character has to *earn their place* through a story beat.

Keep the **core five off the gacha entirely.** Luck is for bonus tenants; the main cast is earned through play.

## Structure

**Act 1 — Settling In.** Establish the house and the loop. Meet the tenants, clear your first fragile gates, feel weak and grow a little. The mystery is only foreshadowing — a gate that behaves wrong, one of Momo's nightmares.

**Act 2 — Cracks.** The gates escalate; a real catastrophe hits close to home; human pressure mounts (Dana's superiors, anti-Gatekin sentiment). Relationships deepen under strain. Choices force loyalties.

**Act 3 — The Threshold.** Everything converges on the house, on the mystery of *why the gates began*, and on whichever bonds you invested in. The resolution ties to the companions you got closest to.

---

## Endings (player-driven)

Two dials the player has been turning all game:

1. **Who you bonded with** → personal/romantic endings (a deep single bond; an ensemble "found family" ending; bittersweet ones for bonds left cold).
2. **What you did about the gates** → world endings (seal the gates forever — safety at the cost of the Gatekin's way home; learn to stabilize them — true coexistence; a sacrifice; a failure ending if the threat is ignored).

Their combination is the replay engine: the same world resolves very differently depending on the heart you brought to it.

---

## One Design Note

Every review of games in this genre says the same thing: too many ads, too little game. Your edge is the **story and the characters** — and now a combat loop with real depth. If players care whether Momo is okay *and* enjoy optimizing their party, you're winning on two axes your competitors ignore. Build the game to be fun first, monetize gently, and let the characters carry it.
